On Leadership and Listening

The most underrated leadership skill is listening without preparing your response. Most leaders listen to reply, not to understand. When a team member brings you a problem, your first instinct is to solve it. But solving too quickly robs people of their own growth and tells them, subtly, that you do not trust their thinking.

In my coaching work I have seen this pattern hundreds of times. The leader who pauses, who asks "what have you already considered?", who sits with silence — that leader builds capability. The leader who answers instantly builds dependency.

Feedback resistance is rarely about the feedback itself. When someone resists all feedback, they are usually protecting something: an identity, a fear of being seen as inadequate, or a past wound from feedback delivered without care. The way through is not better arguments. It is safety. People change when they feel safe enough to consider change, not when they are cornered by evidence.

A practical framework I return to often: Connect, then correct. Before any difficult conversation, invest in the relationship. A person who feels valued can hear hard things. A person who feels judged can hear nothing.

Growth happens at the edge of comfort, not beyond it. Push people too far past their edge and they retreat. Meet them exactly where their courage ends and their fear begins — that is where transformation lives.
